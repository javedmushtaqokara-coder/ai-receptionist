# Kitchen and bath remodeling (residential design-build contractors)

Last checked: 2026-11-23. Sources: Claude's industry knowledge of residential
remodeling sales calls, plus the first client's site
(kitchenandbathmiami.com, Miami-Dade), whose process, cost guide and FAQ
match standard trade practice. Add to this file on later remodeling builds.

## 1. What calls sound like

- **Who calls:** homeowners planning a project (the vast majority), condo
  owners (often with building or HOA rules), landlords and investors,
  realtors, existing clients (project updates, warranty), suppliers and
  subcontractors looking for work, and sales or spam calls.
- **Mood:** excited but cautious. Callers are spending $10k–$300k and are
  often comparing 2–4 contractors. They want to feel the company is
  trustworthy, licensed and responsive. Few calls are urgent; speed of
  callback wins the job.
- **Words callers use:** "redo my kitchen", "gut the bathroom", "walk-in
  shower", "tub to shower conversion", "new cabinets", "quartz", "island",
  "open concept", "take down a wall", "condo", "HOA / building approval",
  "permits", "ballpark", "how long does it take", "free estimate".
- **Sample openings:**
  1. "Hi, I'm looking to remodel my kitchen. Do you do free estimates?"
  2. "How much would it cost to redo a master bathroom, roughly?"
  3. "I have a condo in Brickell. Do you deal with the building's approvals?"
  4. "I submitted a form on your website and haven't heard back."
  5. "You did my bathroom last year and the grout is cracking."

## 2. Call mix

| Reason | Share |
|---|---|
| New project enquiry or estimate request | 50–60% |
| Price or "ballpark" questions | 15% |
| Process questions (timeline, permits, condo rules, warranty) | 10% |
| Existing clients: project updates, scheduling, payments | 10% |
| Warranty claims | under 5% |
| Vendors, subcontractors, job seekers, spam | the rest |

## 3. What callers ask, and what a good answer needs

- **Cost:** published ranges by scope only. Never a quote by phone; the
  real number comes after the in-home visit and itemized estimate.
- **Timeline:** typical durations, plus design and permit time.
- **Process:** free consultation, then design and quote, permits, build,
  and walk-through.
- **Trust:** licensed and insured, pulls own permits, in-house crews,
  warranty, reviews, years in business.
- **Do you do X:** walk-in showers, tub conversions, open concept, flooring,
  condo work, design help, financing.

## 4. What gets booked: the free in-home estimate

Remodelers sell through a consultation. The receptionist's job is to
**qualify and book (or request) the estimate**. Details to collect:
- full name, best phone, email (for the written estimate)
- project type (kitchen, bathroom(s), full home, other) and short scope
- property type (house, condo or townhome) and neighbourhood or city; for
  condos, the building name (approvals, work hours)
- timeline ("as soon as possible", 1–3 months, just planning) and rough
  budget range, asked tactfully and optional
- best days and times for the visit; how they heard about the company

## 5. What gets transferred or escalated

- Existing clients with an active project problem (leak, damage, crew no-show).
- Warranty claims (logged with the details and photos to follow by email).
- Large or high-value projects (full home, luxury) when the owner wants them
  hot-transferred.
- Complaints, legal or insurance matters, and media.

## 6. Emergencies and after hours

Remodelers rarely have true emergencies. The exception is an **active
project** problem: water leaking from new plumbing, or an unsafe or
unsecured site. Treat it as urgent: tell the caller to shut off the water at
the main valve if it's leaking, and take an urgent message. Everything else
can wait for the next business day callback.

## 7. Rules and risks

- Never quote a price, promise a start date or timeline, or approve
  design choices. Ranges only if the business publishes them.
- Never give structural, electrical or plumbing advice, or say a wall can
  be removed.
- Don't confirm licence numbers unless the business has published them.
- Condo projects: don't promise building approval timelines.
- Bilingual markets (Miami, Texas, California): answer in the caller's
  language.
