# HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning)

Last checked: 2026-11-23. Sources: Claude's industry knowledge of residential
and commercial HVAC call handling, plus the first client's site (cowboyair.com,
Dallas-Fort Worth), whose emergency-triage and pricing pages match standard
trade practice. Add to this file on later HVAC builds.

## 1. What calls sound like

- **Who calls:** homeowners (most calls), renters and property managers,
  small-business and commercial facility managers, existing customers about
  a job or warranty, home-warranty companies, suppliers and sales or spam
  calls.
- **Mood:** in peak season (summer heat, winter cold snaps) many callers are
  hot, stressed and in a hurry: "my AC is blowing warm air and it's 95 in
  here". Maintenance and quote callers are calm.
- **Words callers use:** "AC's out", "not cooling", "blowing warm air",
  "freezing up / ice on the line", "unit is leaking", "water in the
  ceiling", "making a loud noise", "breaker keeps tripping", "heater won't
  kick on", "furnace", "heat pump", "thermostat is blank", "tune-up",
  "need a quote on a new system", "how many tons".
- **Sample openings:**
  1. "Hi, my AC stopped working this morning and it's getting really hot in here. Can you send someone today?"
  2. "I'd like to schedule a tune-up before summer."
  3. "How much do you charge just to come out and look at it?"
  4. "I've got a tech coming Thursday and I need to move it to next week."
  5. "There's water dripping from my ceiling under the attic unit."

## 2. Call mix (typical, varies by season)

| Reason | Share |
|---|---|
| Repair / no cooling or no heat (book a diagnostic visit) | 35–45% |
| Price questions (service call fee, repair cost, new system) | 15% |
| Maintenance / tune-up booking | 10–15% |
| Replacement or new-system quotes | 10% |
| Existing appointment: confirm, reschedule, cancel, "where's my tech" | 10% |
| Emergencies after hours | 5% (much higher in heat waves) |
| Warranty, billing, commercial, vendors, spam | the rest |

## 3. What callers ask, and what a good answer needs

- **"How much is a service call / diagnostic?"** The trip or diagnostic fee,
  whether it's credited toward the repair, and the after-hours premium.
- **"How much is a new AC?"** Only ranges, plus a free in-home estimate.
  Never a firm price by phone, because sizing (Manual J) and ductwork
  change it.
- **"How fast can you get here?"** Same-day policy and arrival windows.
- **"Do you service my area / my brand?"** The list of cities, and "all
  makes and models".
- **"Do you offer financing / discounts / maintenance plans?"**
- **"What should I do right now?"** Safe, simple checks only: thermostat
  batteries and settings, breaker reset once, replace a dirty filter, turn
  the system off if there's ice. Never ask callers to open panels or
  handle refrigerant.

## 4. What gets booked

- **Appointment types:** repair or diagnostic visit (most), maintenance or
  tune-up, estimate for a new system, duct cleaning, commercial site visit.
- **Duration:** visits are booked as arrival windows (often 1–4 hours).
  Repairs take 1–3 hours and installs a full day or more. Installs are
  scheduled by the office after an estimate, not by the receptionist.
- **Details needed:** full name, callback phone, service address (with
  city or ZIP for the service area), what's wrong, equipment type and age if
  known, whether anyone vulnerable is in the home, how to access the
  property (gate code, pets), and email if the booking system needs it.
- **Common software:** ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge,
  Service Fusion, GoHighLevel. Small shops often use Google Calendar,
  Calendly or Cal.com.

## 5. What gets transferred or escalated

- True emergencies (see section 6) during or after hours.
- An upset customer, a complaint, or a "your tech damaged..." call.
- Warranty claims and billing disputes.
- Commercial or large-install sales leads: take a detailed message or
  transfer to the owner.
- A caller asking for a specific technician, or for the owner by name.

## 6. Emergencies and after hours

- **Danger to life (911 first):** a gas or rotten-egg smell, smoke, fire,
  burning smell that doesn't fade, or a carbon-monoxide alarm. Tell the
  caller to leave the home and call 911 or the gas company, then offer help.
- **HVAC emergencies (urgent dispatch):** no cooling with indoor
  temperatures above about 85°F and a vulnerable person (elderly, infant,
  medical condition) or pets inside; no heat in freezing weather; water
  leaking through the ceiling; a breaker that trips again after one reset.
- **Can wait until morning:** a noisy unit that still cools, a weak or
  uneven airflow, maintenance, quotes.
- After-hours service usually carries a premium, which is disclosed before
  dispatch.

## 7. Rules and risks

- Never diagnose ("it's your capacitor") or quote a firm repair price. Give
  ranges only if the business publishes them.
- Never tell callers to open electrical panels beyond a single breaker
  reset, touch refrigerant lines, or go into a hot attic.
- State licence holders (for example Texas TACL) may need the licence number
  given on request.
- Don't promise an exact arrival time. Give the window and say the office
  confirms it.
- Discounts (military, senior) and financing ("with approved credit") must
  be described exactly as the business words them.
